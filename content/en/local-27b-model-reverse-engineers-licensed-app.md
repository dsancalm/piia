A local 27-billion-parameter model completed a full reverse-engineering engagement , static disassembly, cryptographic key recovery, vulnerability assessment, and a working bypass , in roughly thirty minutes on a single workstation. The model was Qwen 3.8 27B, served through SGLang with NVFP4 quantization and DFlash2 speculative decoding on a Lenovo ThinkStation PGX equipped with an Nvidia GB10 Grace Blackwell chip, 128 GB of unified memory, and 273 GB/s of bandwidth. Throughput settled around fifty tokens per second on code and reasoning traces.

The target was a commercial application the author had purchased and licensed. The license scheme performs a single online activation, then verifies offline at every launch: it checks an RSA signature, binds to a hardware serial number, validates an embedded revocation list, confirms the binary's own signature, and enforces a signed update path. Qwen rated the design unusually thorough for its class but flagged three weaknesses: an RSA key of awkward size below modern strength, revocation possible only through updates because everything runs offline, and every check living in local code that can be patched.

Working entirely from disassembly , thousands of lines of ARM64 , the model mapped the security functions, located the hidden public RSA key, and reconstructed it byte by byte. Its first candidate passed the signature check but failed an integrity hash. The model detected the mismatch, backtracked, and corrected the key until the hash aligned. The recovered key then verified that the author's legitimate license was signed by the corresponding private key. Only after the complete static analysis did the model produce a functional bypass. The application was never executed until that final demonstration.

Artificial Analysis currently ranks Qwen 3.8 27B as the top open-weight model in the 4B, 40B parameter bracket (135 models tested) with an intelligence index of 52, placing it above several larger proprietary systems on SWE-bench Pro. The model fits in roughly 17 GB of VRAM, making it accessible on high-end consumer hardware. By default it runs its reasoning effort at maximum; even trivial prompts consume hundreds or thousands of tokens, which inflates latency despite the high throughput.

The entire session ran offline. No binary, license, or intermediate analysis left the machine. The model also rejected a jailbreak attempt, verified the code-signing certificate, and identified the actual developer before agreeing to audit and document weaknesses , though it ultimately generated the bypass after completing that audit.
