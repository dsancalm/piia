Spec-Driven Agentic Development (SDAD) positions itself as the fifth software-production paradigm, following Waterfall, Agile, and what the authors label "AI-code." Vu Hung Nguyen and Thanh Nguyen submitted the paper to arXiv on 5 May 2026. The argument: agentic speed does not remove engineering discipline; it moves that discipline upstream into specification precision, explicit gates, and auditable provenance.

The framework rests on four pillars: intent capture, machine-readable specification, agentic synthesis, and independent multi-agent verification under human approval. The authors contrast Human-Agile circa 2020 with Agentic-SDAD circa 2026 across artifacts, cadence, accountability, and security posture. They propose quantitative governance metrics , Ambiguity Tax, Spec Fidelity, SER, and TCI_agentic with a repair multiplier phi , though the paper does not supply concrete formulas or numerical values for any of them.

A staged migration plan and hybrid estimation model are mentioned for pragmatic adoption, but the number of stages, their duration, and entry/exit criteria are not detailed. The paper also describes a metamorphosis of team roles across engineering, QA, platform, and product, yet it does not name specific companies, tools, or empirical case studies that validate the framework. The definition of "AI-code" as a distinct fourth paradigm remains high-level, and the technical implementation of independent multi-agent verification under human approval is not spelled out.

The significance for practitioners is clear: if you work with agents, your leverage shifts from typing faster to writing unambiguous specs and designing verification pipelines that can be audited. The discipline moves from line-by-line code control to specification and gate design.

What is not known:

- Detailed migration stages, timelines, and criteria.
- Concrete industrial evidence: company names, measured outcomes, or toolchains.
