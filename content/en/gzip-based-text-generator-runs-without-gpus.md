You can treat a standard compressor as a language model. The equivalence between compression and prediction means any algorithm that assigns shorter codes to more probable sequences implicitly defines a probability distribution over the next token. Gzip implements DEFLATE with a 32 KiB sliding window. When you feed it a corpus, repetitions inside that window are encoded as cheap back-references. A continuation that reuses substrings already present in the window therefore compresses to fewer bytes. The compressed length of context plus candidate becomes a surrogate for negative log-likelihood: shorter is more probable.

The author packaged this idea into `gzipt`, a single-file Python script that uses only `zlib`. You prime it with a corpus, supply a prompt, and it generates text by beam search over byte sequences. Beam search is essential because the score is an integer byte count; without looking ahead, the quantized steps produce noisy, repetitive output. The scoring context consists of the corpus window plus the recent tail of the prompt and already-generated bytes, not the full history. This prevents the model from simply looping verbatim matches from the distant past.

```bash
gzipt --corpus data/tinyshakespeare.txt --prompt $'MENENIUS:\n' --length 200
```

The output mimics Shakespearean vocabulary and speaker tags because those strings live in the corpus window. The method requires no GPU, no training loop, and no learned parameters. It runs anywhere `zlib` runs, making it a viable baseline for edge devices or teaching environments where a neural model is impractical. The original "Language Modeling is Compression" paper reported poor results with greedy decoding; beam search over a byte-level horizon closes much of the quality gap.

## What is not known

The exact `beam_width` and `horizon` values used in the demonstrations are not disclosed. No quantitative metrics such as perplexity or BLEU scores are provided to compare `gzipt` against small Transformers or n-gram baselines. The size of the Tiny Shakespeare corpus, inference latency per generated byte, and behavior on non-English or larger corpora have not been reported.
